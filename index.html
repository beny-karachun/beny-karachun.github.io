03 Research direction

Make human immunity better at fighting bacteria.

I want to study antimicrobial resistance, especially Staphylococcus aureus, through immune engineering. My interests include gene and cell editing and computational design.

RESEARCH TOOLS

Design. Test. Understand.

I want to help researchers design, test, and explain biological methods with tools that keep the underlying science visible.

01 / PATHOGEN

Staphylococcus aureus

Understand and counter a clinically important pathogen, with antibiotic resistance as the central problem.

02 / HOST

Human immune systems

Improve bacterial detection and targeting, add protective mechanisms, or tune immune responses when necessary.

03 / METHOD

Editing + computation

Explore CRISPR, base editing, engineered immune cells, and machine-learning-guided design as candidate routes.

04 About

I build the tool I wish the lab had.

My training includes microbiology, molecular biology, analytical methods, and quantitative analysis. I combine this laboratory foundation with Python, SQL, and web development.

Alongside my studies, I tutor Python and build websites and tools for students and small businesses. My experience includes technology consulting and work on laboratory and study software.

Why I chose biotechnology

A recurring Staphylococcus aureus infection raised questions about treatment outcomes. Laboratory testing reportedly classified the bacterium as susceptible, yet the infection returned after treatment. This experience does not establish resistance or a specific biological mechanism.

It led me to explore susceptibility testing, persistence, and host–pathogen interactions independently. I began this study about four years before entering the Technion. Those questions helped me choose Biotechnology & Food Engineering.
